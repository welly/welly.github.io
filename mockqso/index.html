The player

  • New QSO makes a contact of the kind chosen and plays it. Any picks a different kind each time.
  • Play and pause with the big button. The buttons beside it step from over to over, and the circular arrow below goes back to the start; tap an over to hear it from there.
  • The Text button sets when the text is shown: Always (each character lights up as it's sent), After each over, or Off.
  • Settings is the button at the top right; the speed at the bottom right opens it too.
  • Share, beside New QSO, copies a link to your settings, to bookmark or send, or to the QSO you're hearing, so someone else can copy exactly the same contact.
  • Keys: Space play or pause, ← → previous or next over, T when to show the text.

Settings

  • Farnsworth spacing sends each character at your Speed but leaves longer gaps between them, so the overall rate is the Overall speed. A good way to copy faster characters before you can keep up with them.
  • Static (QRN) adds crashes from lightning, Other stations (QRM) puts other signals and tuning nearby, and Fading (QSB) makes signals rise and fall.
  • All stations on this tone puts every station on your Tone, as when you tune each one in to your sidetone; otherwise each is on its own pitch, as they'd be on the band.
  • Number of contacts: in activations, pile-ups and contests, how many separate contacts the station on frequency makes, one after another, before signing off. At 1 you hear a single exchange; raise it for a longer session. Ragchews and short QSOs are always one contact.
  • Operators sets how well the stations send; Mixed is like the real bands.

Practising

  • Hide the text and copy on paper or in your head: calls, names, reports, QTHs.
  • Don't stop on a missed letter; pick up the next word.
  • Start a few WPM below your comfortable speed, or turn on Farnsworth spacing for more time between characters.
  • Speed, noise, fading and tone change the QSO you're listening to, so you can go back over it.

About

Each QSO is generated when you ask for one, so no two are the same. Callsigns follow real formats, but the stations, names and QTHs are fictional.

It all runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere, and settings are kept on this device. A long ragchew takes a second or so to make.

Works offline: after your first visit it opens and makes QSOs without a connection. On a phone, add it to your Home Screen (in Safari, Share then Add to Home Screen) to open it like an app.

No sound on an iPhone? Check the volume.

Visits and the kinds of QSO made are counted anonymously, without cookies (GoatCounter). Your callsign, name and QTH are never sent.
